Warrant · privacy

Privacy

Warrant stores your data inside your own Atlassian installation. It has no way to send it anywhere else.

What it stores

The app keeps four kinds of record, all in Atlassian's hosted storage for your site:

  • Warrants — the operation, the frozen list of issue keys, who requested it, who signed it, when it expires, and how many actions remain.
  • Ledger entries — one per change attempted under a warrant, with what was changed and what is needed to reverse it.
  • The approval queue — writes that were refused because they fell outside a warrant, waiting for a person to approve or dismiss them.
  • Nothing else. No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no account identifiers beyond the Atlassian account IDs involved in a warrant.

What it reads

To show what a permission would touch, the app reads Jira issues, comments, users and linked work through the scopes it declares: read:jira-work, write:jira-work, read:jira-user, and three read-only Teamwork Graph scopes for Jira work items, external pull requests and Atlassian goals. These reads happen only when a card is opened or a warrant is checked.

What it never does

  • It does not send data off Atlassian. The manifest declares no outbound network permission, so the app has no egress at all. There is no server of ours to send anything to.
  • It does not retain data after uninstall. Removing the app removes its Atlassian-hosted storage with it.

Personal data

Atlassian account IDs and display names appear on warrants and ledger entries because a record of who granted authority is the point of the product. No email addresses, no IP addresses and no content beyond what Jira already holds are collected.
